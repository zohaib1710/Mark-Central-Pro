EIN registration

Prepare for the federal tax ID step with clarity.

An Employer Identification Number can be an important part of business administration, depending on your entity and circumstances.

What an EIN is

An EIN is a federal tax identification number.

EIN stands for Employer Identification Number. It is issued by the Internal Revenue Service and may be used by businesses and other entities for federal tax administration.

Whether a business needs an EIN can depend on its structure, employees, tax obligations, banking needs, and other circumstances. Requirements and application options may change.

Common uses

Why a business may need an EIN.

An EIN does not replace every registration or compliance obligation.

Federal tax administration

Businesses may use an EIN when filing certain federal tax returns or handling federal tax obligations.

Hiring employees

Businesses with employees commonly need an EIN for employment-tax reporting and related administration.

Business operations

Banking, licensing, vendor, payroll, and recordkeeping requirements may call for an EIN depending on the situation.

Before you apply

Organize the business information first.

For a new LLC or corporation, state formation generally comes before an EIN application. Keep the entity’s legal name, responsible-party details, address, and formation records accurate and consistent.

For international founders

Application options may differ.

A non-U.S. owner may be able to obtain an EIN for a qualifying U.S. business. The available application route and required information can depend on the business and the responsible party.

Do not send taxpayer-identification information through a general inquiry form. Additional information may be needed later through the applicable official process.
